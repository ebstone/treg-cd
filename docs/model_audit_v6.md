# Audit of the IBD CEA v6 workbooks

Findings from a direct read of `IBD_CEA_v6_PSA.xlsm` and `IBD_CEA_v6_Univariate_Sensitivity_Analysis.xlsm` (formulas and cached values). Cell references are given so each can be checked independently. Severity reflects likely effect on reported results. Direction of bias is stated where it can be determined.

Extracted from `docs/analysis_plan.md` v1.0, Appendix A, so it stands as its own record of what changed and why as the rebuild in `R/` proceeds. Cross-referenced from the analysis plan's Section 0.2 and Section 15 decisions.

## A.1 Errors

**A1 — Costs are discounted against cumulative deaths, not elapsed time. [High severity]**
In all three Markov sheets, the discounted-cost column uses `=AQn*(1/(1+discount)^Nn)`. Column N holds *cumulative conventional-therapy deaths*; elapsed time in years is column A (`=Bn*(8/52)`). The QALY column is correct (`=ADn*(1/(1+discount)^An)`). At cycle 7 the exponent applied to costs is 0.343 (deaths) instead of 1.077 (years); at the final cycle the implied discount factor is ≈0.899 rather than the correct ≈0.833.
*Effect:* costs are systematically under-discounted in every arm. Because the comparators' costs are recurring and Treg's are front-loaded, this inflates comparator costs relative to Treg — **biases in favour of Treg**.

**A2 — Base-case Treg "Surgery" transition row appears column-shifted. [High severity]**
`Input Parameters` C34:C39 give Surgery → M-S 0.042, M-SR 0.0276, Mild 0.228, Remission 0, Surgery 0.7024. Ustekinumab's corresponding row is 0.0405 / 0.0267 / 0.0267 / 0.6776 / 0.2283, and the workbook's own Alpha (10%) and Beta (30%) Treg sheets give Remission 0.706 / 0.763 and Surgery-self 0.205 / 0.160. Under the documented 20% rule the base case should be approximately Remission 0.73, Surgery-self 0.18, Mild 0.03. The values for Mild, Remission and Surgery appear to have been misplaced.
*Effect:* Treg patients entering the Surgery state largely remain there, accruing the surgery state cost ($34,679/cycle) and the lowest utility. **Biases against Treg.** Correcting it will improve Treg's reported cost-effectiveness; the magnitude must be quantified.

**A3 — Second Treg dose is charged to the wrong cohort. [Moderate]**
`TREG Markov` AJ10 = `SUM(AF10:AI10)+IF(treg_total_doses>1, H16*cost_treg_dose, 0)`. The cost is charged in row 10 (cycle 7, ≈1.08 years — correct timing) but multiplied by `H16`, the number alive in the Treg track at row 16 (cycle 13, ≈2 years). It should be `H10`.
*Effect:* undercounts the patients receiving dose 2. **Biases in favour of Treg.** Moot if the single-dose base case is adopted.

**A4 — Dollar years are mixed across arms. [Moderate]**
Treg health-state costs are un-inflated 2017 values (`costs_treg_MSR` 217, `costs_treg_mild` 91, `costs_treg_remission` 10) while ustekinumab and infliximab use 2025 values ($282.86, $118.62, $13.04). See analysis plan §8.
*Effect:* Treg's non-drug maintenance costs understated by ≈23%. **Biases in favour of Treg** (small in absolute terms relative to drug costs).

**A5 — Infliximab M-SR state cost appears to double-count administration. [Low–moderate]**
`Input Parameters`: `costs_ifx_MS` = dose + 402, `costs_ifx_mild` = dose + 238, `costs_ifx_remission` = dose + 133 — each equal to the health-state cost plus one $119.36 administration fee. But `costs_ifx_MSR` = dose + 522 ≈ 402.22 + 119.36, i.e. the M-S figure *plus a second* administration fee.
*Effect:* overstates infliximab costs in the M-SR state. Biases against infliximab.

**A6 — Infliximab induction cost differs between the two workbooks. [High severity for the PSA]**
Univariate workbook: `cost_inx_MS` = $3,563/patient (hardcoded; consistent with three induction infusions at weeks 0, 2 and 6). PSA workbook: `=IF(psa_switch=0,1068,psa_costs_ifx_dose)+…` = $1,372/patient — a single infusion.
*Effect:* the PSA understates infliximab induction cost by ≈$2,191/patient. The probabilistic results are therefore **not** the stochastic counterpart of the deterministic results. Any PSA-based claim in the current manuscript is affected.

**A7 — The Economically Justifiable Price worksheet is disconnected from the model. [High severity]**
`Economically Justifiable Price` D9 = 26.48 (QALY Treg), D10 = 25.73 (QALY IFX), D15 = 73,125.2 (Costs IFX) — all hardcoded. The QALY values appear nowhere else in the workbook (the model's discounted per-patient QALYs are ≈4.07 and ≈3.95). The sheet returns EJP = $185,625, while the manuscript reports $95,382. The "graph" column additionally computes `(price − Costs_IFX)/ΔQALY`, treating the x-axis variable as *total cost*, not price.
*Effect:* the reported EJP cannot be reproduced from the workbook. This number must be recomputed from scratch.

**A8 — The conventional-therapy track omits health-state costs. [Moderate]**
`costs_ct_MS` = `costs_ct_MSR` = `costs_ct_mild` = `costs_ct_remission` = 88, i.e. a flat per-cycle cost in every non-surgical state. In Aliyev, the CT cycle cost ($67, 2017 USD) is the CT *drug* cost, applied **in addition to** health-state costs.
*Effect:* understates CT-track costs in all arms. Because the share of the cohort on the CT track differs sharply by arm (≈79% for UST, ≈76% for IFX, ≈34% for Treg at induction), the bias is not symmetric — it **favours the comparators**, partially offsetting A1 and A4.

**A9 — Surgery is charged as a full episode cost in every cycle of occupancy. [Moderate]**
`costs_*_sur` ≈ $35,518, an HCUP colectomy episode cost, is multiplied by the Surgery-state occupancy each cycle. With an 8-week probability of remaining in Surgery of 0.228 (and 0.702 in the Treg base case, see A2), a meaningful share of the cohort is charged a full surgical episode repeatedly. Aliyev used a per-cycle state cost derived from PMPM data ($884, 2017 USD).
*Effect:* likely overstates surgical costs in all arms; interacts badly with A2. Needs an explicit decision: tunnel state with a one-time episode cost, or a per-cycle state cost.

*Resolved 2026-08-04:* **per-cycle state cost, not a one-time episode cost** — matching how every other health state in Aliyev's design is costed, and matching Aliyev's Costs Assumption #1 (Suppl. Table 1) that health-state costs are constant across all biologics and CT. Aliyev's Surgery state is not a colectomy proxy: it is explicitly documented as covering "all surgeries and procedures" (Lichtenstein et al. 2005), most performed outpatient, costed via the Malone et al. severe-fulminant PMPM claims category — chosen only because it was the sole category with reported inpatient costs, not because Surgery-state occupants are assumed to be undergoing major inpatient operations. The native 2-week Aliyev matrix (Suppl. Table 4) confirms the state is short-staying by design (Surgery→Remission = 0.868, Surgery→Surgery = 0.098 per 2-week cycle; expected occupancy ≈1.1 cycles), consistent with a recurring claims-average cost rather than a one-time DRG charge.

Base case now uses Aliyev's own per-cycle Surgery figure ($884, 2017 USD) inflated by the same ≈1.3035 factor already applied to Aliyev's other per-cycle costs (Decision 5, analysis_plan.md §8) → **$1,152.29 (2025 USD)**, applied identically across TREG/UST/IFX/CT in `data/processed/model_health_state_costs.csv`. The HCUP colectomy figure is retained in `data/raw/cms_asp_and_hcup_cost_sources.csv` for provenance and as a candidate input for a future episode-cost structural scenario, but is no longer used in the base case.

**Open item carried forward:** Aliyev's own materials do not cleanly reconcile on this figure — the manuscript appendix worked example states $884 (2017 USD) while Suppl. Table 2 lists the underlying Severe-Fulminant PMPM at $1,475 (nominally "2008 USD," though the table's footnote states all costs were converted to 2017 USD). $884 was used here for consistency with how the model already treats Aliyev's other four per-cycle costs (M-S/Mild/Remission/CT), which come from the same appendix worked-example section rather than Table 2's raw PMPM inputs — but the derivation from $1,475 (or from the underlying $374/$123/$111 building blocks) to $884 is not independently reconstructable from the materials on hand. Verify against the original Aliyev appendix table image, or with the Aliyev co-authors if reachable, before this goes to submission. This is the same unresolved reconciliation already noted for M-S/Mild/Remission/CT (data_dictionary.md, analysis_plan.md §8) — Surgery does not add a new gap, it inherits the existing one.

## A.2 Reproducibility and documentation gaps

**A10 — Treg transition probabilities are hardcoded and the documented method does not reproduce them.**
All Treg values in `Input Parameters` C10:C39 (and the Alpha/Beta blocks) are literal numbers, not formulas referencing ustekinumab. The **maintenance** rows are approximately consistent with the documented 20% rule. The **induction** row is not: base-case M-S → M-S is 0.3368 against ustekinumab's 0.791, a ≈57% relative reduction rather than 20%. The Alpha/Beta/base induction values are linear in the stated advantage parameter but imply a zero-advantage intercept of ≈0.421, not 0.791. Since the induction split determines what fraction of the cohort ever reaches the biologic track at all, this is the single most influential set of numbers in the model, and it is currently unreproducible from the manuscript's stated method.
*Action:* re-derive in code, or document the actual derivation. Do not carry these values forward unexamined.

**A11 — Cycle-length conversion cannot be verified.** All 8-week transition probabilities are hardcoded; the DEALE intermediate calculations are not in the file. See analysis plan §7.3.

**A12 — No half-cycle correction. [RESOLVED 2026-08-05]** Cohort state occupancy is applied at cycle boundaries with no correction.
*Resolution:* `R/04_costs_utilities.R`'s new `half_cycle_weights()` applies the standard trapezoidal-rule correction (CHEERS 2022 item 17; analysis_plan.md §4.1's "Apply (life-table or Simpson's 1/3)" recommendation) to every continuously-accruing, state-occupancy-driven cost and QALY calculation (`trace_qalys()`/`trace_costs()`, and the SDR-specific accrual in `attach_sdr_costs_utilities()`) — both endpoints of a trace (baseline and terminal occupancy) weighted at 0.5, every interior timepoint at full weight, approximating each cycle's true continuous occupancy by the straight-line average of its two bounding timepoints. Deliberately NOT applied to one-time or discrete-event costs (induction/dose acquisition, maintenance drug doses on their specific dosing cycles) — those aren't a continuously-accruing quantity the correction is modelling, and applying it there would incorrectly discount a dose actually given at the first or last cycle. Default `half_cycle_correction = TRUE` from `R/04` up through `R/05`'s `run_comparator_arm_lifetime()`/`run_treg_arm_lifetime()`; `FALSE` reproduces the exact pre-fix figures for comparability. **Every deterministic and probabilistic result produced before this fix is superseded** (same class of change as A16 and the biosimilar re-pricing) — the shift is small in relative terms (the correction only discounts the weight of 2 of `HORIZON_CYCLES_6YR`/`HORIZON_CYCLES_LIFETIME`'s many rows, from 1.0 to 0.5) but is real and, here, directional: since occupancy and utility/cost values are always non-negative, halving both endpoints' weight can only ever REDUCE a trace's summed total relative to the uncorrected figure, never raise it — strictly, given the baseline row (cycle 0, 100% cohort mass in a non-Death state) always contributes positive utility/cost mass. QALYs and costs both fall (very slightly) for every arm; downstream quantities computed from their DIFFERENCE (incremental QALYs, ICERs, π*, EJP) move by whatever the two arms' own reductions net out to, not necessarily in one uniform direction. Full test suite: 461 assertions before this fix; regenerated afterward (README.md's Status section has the current count).

**A13 — Cycle count and cycles-per-year are misstated in the manuscript.** The workbook runs 40 cycles (rows 4–43); the manuscript states 39. Forty 8-week cycles = 6.15 years, so the workbook is internally consistent and the manuscript text is wrong. Separately, Supplement 1 states "# cycles per year 6.15"; the correct figure, used by the workbook, is 6.5 (52/8). The 6.15 is the horizon in years, not a rate.

**A14 — The PSA samples only seven parameters.** `PSA` rows 3–12: five utility parameters (Uniform over their CI bounds) and two drug unit costs (Gamma). No transition probability, no efficacy parameter, no health-state cost and no utility-for-Treg-specific-state is sampled. ICERs are computed per iteration (column R) rather than incremental net monetary benefit.
*Effect:* the reported PSA characterises uncertainty in utilities and drug prices only. It cannot support EVPI or EVPPI, which are undefined over parameters held fixed. Rebuilding the PSA is a prerequisite for the primary endpoints of the redesign, not an optional improvement.

**A15 — Two different dose-cost figures for the same drug disagree by a consistent, unexplained factor. [Low–moderate, found 2026-08-04, RESOLVED 2026-08-05]** `data/processed/model_dose_costs_and_psa_ranges.csv` carries two columns for both the TREG and IFX dose-cost rows: `value_usd` and `psa_base`. For TREG these are $18,908.74 vs $19,917; for IFX, $1,014.20 vs $1,068. Both pairs are in the same ~0.9494× ratio (0.94938 and 0.94963 respectively) — too close to be coincidental, implying a systematic, undocumented adjustment applied to `value_usd` (or omitted from it) somewhere in the source workbook's "Treg Cost official" sheet.
*Effect:* whichever figure is wrong overstates or understates both Treg's and IFX's drug costs by ~5%. `psa_base` is the one used going forward (`R/04_costs_utilities.R`'s `treg_dose_cost()`): it matches the ten Ham derivation chain (`model_tenham_derived_treg_dose_cost.csv`) and analysis_plan.md §7.1 item 12's own cited $19,917 figure exactly, whereas `value_usd`'s derivation is not reconstructable from any material in this repository.
*Resolution (2026-08-05, `docs/decision_resolutions_2026-08-05.md` §2):* the ~0.949389× factor is a *shared* multiplicative shock (TREG: 18,908.7355/19,916.7498 = 0.949389; IFX: 1,014.2007/1,068.266 (unrounded) = 0.949389, identical to six figures) applied across a price row in the source workbook — a common-random-number PSA draw or a scenario multiplier left live in the sheet, from two build-ups (a ten Ham manufacturing derivation and a CMS ASP figure) with no shared origin that could produce the same factor independently. `psa_base` is independently traceable end to end for both drugs (TREG to the ten Ham chain to the cent; IFX to ASP) and is confirmed correct; `value_usd` is a downstream transform of a snapshot artefact, same class as A16. **Closed — no manuscript limitations sentence needed; it was never a modelling choice.**

**A16 — The Remission utility actually used in the model doesn't match either the PSA distribution's stated centre or the cited literature figure. [Material, found 2026-08-05 building `R/06_psa.R`, RESOLVED 2026-08-05]** `data/processed/model_health_utilities.csv` (the file `R/04_costs_utilities.R`'s `load_health_state_utilities()` used to read, and every deterministic result in `R/05_deterministic_results.R` therefore ran on) gave Remission utility as **0.9554396356**. `data/processed/model_psa_parameter_distributions.csv` and `model_utility_ratios_and_psa.csv` — the workbook's own PSA specification for the same quantity — both cite **0.82** as the base case (matching analysis_plan.md §7.1 item 19's own citation: Aliyev / NICE TA352), with a Uniform(0.66, 0.98) range around it. 0.9554 sits inside that range (barely, near the top) but is not the range's own stated centre by any reasonable reading — a ~16.5% relative gap from the cited base value, in the same undocumented-workbook-arithmetic family as A6/A7/A15, not a rounding artifact. The same relationship holds down the entire multiplicative utility chain (§10.2's "must be sampled as a chain, as the current workbook correctly does"): each of Mild/M-SR/M-S/Surgery in `model_health_utilities.csv` reproduces exactly as (previous state's utility) × (that state's own ratio in `model_utility_ratios_and_psa.csv`) — so the chain's *mechanics* are internally consistent and verified correct; only its Remission starting point disagreed with the cited literature base.
*Resolution (2026-08-05, `docs/decision_resolutions_2026-08-05.md` §1):* 0.9554 is a probabilistic sensitivity analysis draw captured live in the workbook snapshot, not a parameter — four independent lines of evidence: (a) the whole chain reproduces to full double precision starting from 0.9554 (Remission → Mild → M-SR → M-S → Surgery, each step matching `model_health_utilities.csv`'s own value to 10 significant figures); (b) every one of the five chained inputs (Remission and its 4 ratios) sits at a scattered, non-base percentile of its own stated Uniform range (92.3, 27.7, 5.4, 1.2, 56.9 — no systematic transform produces that pattern; five independent uniform draws do); (c) all five carry ~16 significant figures, the signature of a volatile `=lo+RAND()*(hi-lo)` cell, not a typed constant; (d) only the 0.82 chain (not the 0.9554 chain) reproduces the manuscript's own published utility table exactly (0.82→0.7298→0.6495→0.5651, matching published 0.73/0.65/0.57). **0.82 is correct.** `model_health_utilities.csv` is retired as a base-case input (kept in the repo with a provenance note, for traceability only); `R/04_costs_utilities.R`'s `load_health_state_utilities()` now derives the deterministic utility vector from `model_psa_parameter_distributions.csv`'s sourced base values via the same multiplicative chain `R/06_psa.R` already samples around, so the deterministic base case is by construction the central draw of the PSA rather than a separately maintained figure. `tests/testthat/test-parameter-provenance.R` (added 2026-08-05) makes this class of defect (A15, A16: a `data/processed/` snapshot value used deterministically without a traceable source) a permanent, mechanical acceptance-test failure rather than something that has to be rediscovered by hand again.
*Effect, upgraded to material:* correcting this lowers every arm's QALYs (~8–15%), lowers incremental QALYs, raises ICERs, raises the required cure fraction π\*, and lowers the EJP — **every deterministic result produced before this fix (`R/05_deterministic_results.R`'s base case, `R/08_ejp.R`'s EJP) is superseded, not merely revised.** The manuscript's previously-published QALYs (3.90–4.07 over 6.15 years, mean utility ≈0.70 after discounting) are consistent with the retired 0.9554 chain and *not* with the corrected 0.82 chain — flagged here as a material finding, not low–moderate, because it means results generated before this fix must be regenerated, not footnoted.

**A17 — Induction transition probabilities were applied for one 2-week cycle instead of the full induction period. [Blocking, found 2026-08-05 in external peer review, RESOLVED 2026-08-06]** `R/00_derive_transition_probs.R`'s `load_published_induction()` read Aliyev Supplementary Table 3's Moderate-Severe row directly as the end-of-induction occupancy split, on the stated rationale that the other rows (Moderate-Severe Responder/Mild/Remission → self, probability 1) prove "this is a single-step absorbing partition, not a repeated-cycle process." That inference does not hold: those absorbing rows are exactly what Aliyev's own Induction Assumption #3 specifies for a MULTI-cycle induction with within-phase absorbing states ("any patient that transitions out of the Moderate-Severe state during induction remains in the new state until the end of induction"). Appendix S2's own worked example is explicit that Table 3's values are per-2-week-cycle rates derived FROM a trial endpoint, not the endpoint itself: "Ustekinumab week 6 remission probability → rate = −ln(1−0.349)/(6/52) = 3.720/yr → Probability (2 weeks) = 1 − exp(−3.720/26) = **0.133** ... a 2-week Moderate-Severe to Remission transition probability" — 0.133 is exactly Table 3's published UST entry, confirming it must be applied 3 times (UST, week-6 endpoint) or 2 times (IFX/ADA, week-4 endpoint), not once, to reproduce the trial result it was derived from: `1−(1−0.133)^3 = 0.348` vs. published UNITI week-6 remission 0.349; `1−(1−0.203)^2 = 0.365` vs. an independently-computed ADA week-4 remission of ~0.360 (0.50 response × 0.72 remitter:responder, `data/raw/aliyev2019_appendixS1_table2_parameters.csv`).

*Effect:* this was a first-order error in every comparator arm, not a rounding issue. Reading the 2-week rate as the terminal split understated UST's true end-of-induction responder pool by ≈2.4× (0.2085 as coded vs. 0.5044 corrected) and IFX/ADA's by ≈1.75× (0.2445 vs. 0.4291), which (a) **reversed the UST/IFX ordering** — IFX had the larger responder pool as coded, UST does once corrected, which matters because `best_comparator_nmb()` solves the EJP against whichever comparator has the highest NMB at a given λ; (b) understated comparator maintenance drug cost by the same ~2×/1.75× factor, since only responders enter the biologic maintenance track and the EJP is approximately the NPV of displaced comparator drug spend (§4.2); (c) shrank Treg's own SDR pool non-proportionally, since π is conditional on Remission occupancy on the biologic track at the week-56 landmark (`R/03_cure_fraction_module.R:145-149`) — a ~2.4×-too-small responder pool yields a ~2.4×-too-small SDR pool for the same π, biasing π\* upward. **Every deterministic and probabilistic number produced before this fix is superseded**, same class as A16 and the biosimilar re-pricing.

*Resolution (2026-08-06):* `load_published_induction()` now builds each therapy's full 4-state induction matrix (`INDUCTION_STATES`, `R/00_derive_transition_probs.R`) and runs it through `simulate_cohort()` (moved to `R/utils/transition_matrix.R` so R/00 can call it) for `INDUCTION_CYCLES[[therapy]]` cycles (UST = 3, week-6 endpoint; IFX/ADA = 2, week-4 endpoint), reading the terminal row as the true end-of-induction split — rather than reading Table 3's Moderate-Severe row as a 1-cycle terminal value. No change was needed to `R/01_decision_tree.R`'s `run_decision_tree()` or any downstream caller: the fix is entirely upstream, at the point Table 3 is loaded, so every consumer of `load_published_induction()`'s output (R/05, R/06, R/08, the refractory-multiplier scenario) inherits the corrected split automatically. `tests/testthat/test-external-validity.R` (new, per M9's own recommendation in the same peer review) asserts the acceptance test the review specified: end-of-induction Remission occupancy reproduces UNITI's published week-6 remission (0.349, ±0.03) for UST and an independently-computed week-4 estimate (~0.36, ±0.02) for IFX/ADA, as an EXTERNAL face-validity check rather than an internal round-trip.

*Follow-up:* B2 (PSA/EVPI/EVPPI/EJP running at the 6.15-year horizon rather than the lifetime horizon §4.3 adopts as base case) was fixed alongside this commit — see README.md's Status section for the horizon-focus change and its 2026-08-06 live-run confirmation. B3 (the relapse hazard h is never sampled in the PSA, so EVPPI subset B / A∪B stay unanswerable) was out of scope for that pass by explicit direction, and was worked in parallel the same day — closed as **A19** below.

**A18 — Surgery-state transitions have two irreconcilable published sources; only one is wired into the model, and S12's counterintuitive finding depends on it. [Moderate, found 2026-08-05 in external peer review as "R2", RESOLVED as a sensitivity check 2026-08-06]** `data/raw/aliyev2019_appendixS1_table2_parameters.csv` (sourced, never wired into any transition matrix) has an 8-week Surgery row (self-loop 0.34) that does not reconcile with `data/raw/aliyev2019_appendixS2_table4_maintenance_transition_probabilities.csv`'s 2-week Surgery row (self-loop 0.0976, 86.8% one-step return to Remission — the one actually used by every arm in this model): compounding the 2-week self-loop forward 4x implies ≈9.1e-5 at 8 weeks, nowhere near 0.34; converting 0.34 down to 2 weeks implies a self-loop around 0.76, an order of magnitude above 0.0976. Table 2 describes a Surgery state patients stay in far longer than the one this model actually runs on. This mattered beyond just an unreconciled data pair because S12 (`docs/analysis_plan.md` §6.2, the non-cured hazard-ratio scenario) found a genuinely counterintuitive result — strengthening the "advantaged" hazard ratio very slightly LOWERS aggregate QALYs — traced specifically to Table 4's own Surgery row having an unusually fast one-step return to Remission; that finding is only as trustworthy as Table 4's own Surgery dynamics.

*Resolution (2026-08-06):* not resolved by picking a winner between the two tables (neither is clearly wrong, and Table 2's own extract is missing enough information — see below — that it can't simply replace Table 4 as a new base case). Instead, built as an explicit sensitivity check: `R/utils/surgery_row_sensitivity.R`'s `build_table2_surgery_row()` reconstructs a native-2-week-equivalent Surgery row from Table 2's 8-week figures, via the same constant-hazard cycle-length conversion Aliyev's own Appendix S2 worked example uses (applied to the aggregate "leaves Surgery" probability, then re-split across destinations in their original relative proportions — not a full matrix fractional-power conversion, which this project has no existing utility for). Three assumptions fill gaps Table 2's own extract leaves open (it reports only 3 of the row's destinations, summing to 0.475, not 1): unreported residual mass (0.525 at 8 weeks) assigned to Remission, matching Table 4's own Remission-dominated pattern; the combined "Mild/M-SR" figure split evenly, matching Table 4's own even split; Surgery-to-Death assigned 0 (Table 2 reports none, and Table 4's own figure is already negligible). All three stated explicitly, not silently assumed — see that file's own header for the full reasoning. `R/09_scenarios.R`'s new `run_scenario_r2_surgery_sensitivity()` re-runs S12's own hazard-ratio grid against this alternative row alongside the real Table-4-sourced one.

**Finding: the sign flips.** Under Table 4 (the real, sourced base case), QALYs fall very slightly as the advantage strengthens (S12's own documented finding, unchanged). Under Table 2's much stickier reconstructed Surgery row, QALYs instead RISE as the advantage strengthens — the "intuitively expected" direction. Cost falls monotonically under either source (Surgery remains expensive to enter regardless of how sticky it is once entered), so NMB still rises under either source — the review's own recommended caveat ("don't report the counterintuitive finding as settled if the sign flips") is now the correct standing position on S12's QALY-direction claim specifically, not merely a hypothetical risk. `output/tables/scenario_r2_surgery_sensitivity.csv` (via `analysis/run_full_analysis.R` step 10/11) carries both sources side by side with an explicit `qaly_direction` column. `tests/testthat/test-surgery-row-sensitivity.R` covers the reconstruction, the matrix-substitution mechanics, and the sign-flip finding itself.
**A19 — The post-cure relapse hazard was assumed to be zero everywhere that produced a headline number. [Blocking, found 2026-08-05 in external peer review as "B3", RESOLVED 2026-08-06]** (Numbered A19, not A18: R2's Surgery-row finding above claimed A18 first, in a commit landed on `main` the same morning while this fix was in progress.) The review reported this as a PSA gap: `R/06_psa.R`'s `run_psa()` hardcoded `relapse_hazard_annual = 0` on every draw, so EVPPI subset B (durability) and the A∪B durability block did not exist and Aim 3 ("does the durability block or the cost block carry higher EVPPI") was unanswerable. Tracing every call site while fixing it found the defect was **larger than reported and had a second, deterministic half**: `headroom_pi_star()` and `headroom_frontier()` (`R/05_deterministic_results.R`) and `ejp_deterministic()` and `ejp_frontier()` (`R/08_ejp.R`) *also* defaulted `relapse_hazard_annual = 0`, and `analysis/run_full_analysis.R`'s primary pipeline calls all four without passing it. Every π\* and every EJP figure this project has ever published was therefore computed on the assumption that Treg's cure is permanent, for every patient, forever.

This was not a placeholder awaiting data. The h-sweep work of 2026-08-05 had already recorded `DEFAULT_RELAPSE_DURATION_YEARS <- 10` as the base-case anchor (PolTREG's 7–12 year multi-year plateau; "nothing published supports permanence"), had already built `duration_to_hazard()` to convert it, and `R/03_cure_fraction_module.R`'s own docstring had already stated in as many words that **"zero relapse hazard is the single MOST favourable assumption available to the intervention"** — the exact framing error that memo existed to correct. The sweep was built as an add-on; the functions that actually produce results were never switched over to it, and the README of the day recorded that as a feature ("No existing function's default behaviour changed — every prior call site with `relapse_hazard_annual` left implicit still gets 0"). A16's own lesson (a deterministic base case maintained separately from the PSA's centre drifts) applied here in a form nobody checked for.

*Effect:* first-order on every Treg-involving output, in the anti-conservative direction throughout. At the lifetime horizon and Treg's sourced acquisition price ($19,916.75), the required durable cure fraction π\* rises from **27.5% / 20.5% / 16.8%** (h = 0) to **72.4% / 54.3% / 44.6%** at WTP $50k / $100k / $150k — still feasible at all three thresholds, but a materially harder target. Deterministic EJP at π = 1 and WTP $150k falls from $99,995 to $40,077; the probabilistic EJP median at $150k falls from $49,631 to roughly $24,000 (300-draw check; the authoritative figure needs the full 10,000-draw run). **Every deterministic and probabilistic Treg number produced before this fix is superseded**, same class as A16, A17 and the biosimilar re-pricing. Two exceptions, both structural rather than lucky: (i) the π = 0 rows — `base_case_results.csv`, `base_case_results_by_horizon.csv`, `refractory_scenario_results.csv`, the S12 table — are bit-identical, because `run_treg_arm()` seeds the SDR track with `remission_mass * π` and every later update multiplies that mass, so at π = 0 no relapse hazard can change the trace; (ii) every UST/IFX/ADA figure everywhere is untouched, since h enters the model only through Treg's own SDR state, which no comparator arm has.

*Resolution (2026-08-06), both halves:* **(a) Deterministic.** All four functions above now default `relapse_hazard_annual = duration_to_hazard(DEFAULT_RELAPSE_DURATION_YEARS)`. h = 0 remains reachable by passing it explicitly, which is what `headroom_frontier_by_duration()`'s T = ∞ row already does — an explicitly labelled upper bound inside a sweep, which is the honest place for that assumption. The π = 0 call sites keep their explicit `= 0` (inert, as above) with the reasoning recorded inline so it is not "corrected" later by mistake. **(b) Probabilistic.** `run_psa()` samples h per draw from a Gamma distribution — the standard health-economics convention for a *rate* as opposed to a probability (Beta) or a multinomial row (Dirichlet): Briggs, Claxton & Sculpher, *Decision Modelling for Health Economic Evaluation* (Oxford University Press, 2006), §4.3 — with its **mean fixed at ln(2)/10 = 0.0693/yr**, the same 10-year anchor the deterministic default now uses, so the PSA is the stochastic counterpart of the deterministic base case by construction rather than a second maintained figure. Shape 2 (CV = 0.71) implies a median SDR duration of 11.9 years, an interquartile range of 7.4–20.8 years, and puts the sweep's pessimistic endpoint T = 2 years at the 99.95th percentile of h — in the tail, not in the body. Shape > 1 forces the density to zero at h = 0, so permanence has probability zero under the prior, which is the point; an Exponential prior would instead make near-permanence the modal outcome. The full argument, including the tension that no mean-anchored distribution can bracket T = 2 without assigning ~26% of its mass beyond T = 20 (in hazard space the sweep grid is asymmetric about its own anchor: T = 2 is 5× the anchor hazard, T = 20 only 0.5×), is on `sample_relapse_hazard_draws()`'s docstring. `R/07_evpi_evppi.R` gains subsets **B** and **A∪B** on that basis, and the all-parameters row becomes A∪B∪C∪E.

*Aim 3, now answerable and answered* (300-draw lifetime-horizon check, WTP $150k, pending the authoritative run): subset A (π) $2,563, subset B (h) $947, **durability block A∪B $3,277 = 93% of total EVPI**, against cost block C (Treg price) $365 = 10%. The durability block carries an order of magnitude more EVPPI than the cost block, and B alone still beats C — the answer to the falsifiable question §2's Aim 3 poses, which could not previously be given at all. Note that the A∪B∪C∪E row scores *below* its own subsets; that is the PCA-dilution artifact already documented on `evppi_by_subset()` (8 raw parameters compressed into 2 components), not a new finding — A∪B itself is 2 raw dimensions and is left unreduced, which is exactly why it is the trustworthy row to lead Aim 3 with.

## A.3 What the audit implies for the reported results

The manuscript's base-case numbers (IFX $73,124 / 3.951 QALYs; Treg $80,855 / 4.068; UST $118,672 / 3.904; ICER $65,918; NMB $519,527 / $529,389 / $466,926 per 1,000 patients) are exactly reproducible from the univariate workbook's cached values, so the reported figures are internally faithful to that file. The issue is not arithmetic slippage between model and manuscript; it is that the file itself contains the defects above.

Note also that the NMB column in `Results` carries both a live formula and a hardcoded "Static" column holding precisely the manuscript's reported values, while the live formula in the PSA workbook returns different numbers. Whatever the history, the practical consequence is that the manuscript's results are a frozen snapshot that the live workbook no longer reproduces. This, more than any individual defect, is the argument for rebuilding in scripted, version-controlled code.
